# Day 1 — Electronics & Rover Foundations

## Overview

Day 1 of the ATLAS Summer Tech School 2026 introduced the foundations of the Robotic Lunar Rover project.

The day focused on understanding the rover's hardware, basic electronics, embedded systems, and the relationship between physical circuits and programming.

![Day 1 — ATLAS Summer Tech School](../DAY%201.jpg)

*Day 1 — Introduction to electronics, Arduino, and the Robotic Rover project.*

## Rover Hardware Introduction

We were introduced to several components that would be used throughout the rover project, including:

- ESP32 microcontroller
- Geared DC motors
- L298N motor driver
- LiPo battery
- Breadboard
- Jumper wires
- Electronic components and sensors

The ESP32 was introduced as the microcontroller used to interface with electronic components, while the L298N was introduced for controlling the rover's motors.

## Electronics Foundations

The practical work began with fundamental electronics concepts and breadboard prototyping.

Topics covered included:

- Breadboard structure and power rails
- Digital and analog signals
- Resistors
- LED polarity
- Basic circuit connections
- ESP32 power and ground connections
- GPIO concepts

Understanding these fundamentals was important before moving toward more complex rover control.

## First Circuit

A basic LED circuit was assembled and connected with the ESP32.

This provided an introduction to the relationship between:

**Microcontroller → GPIO → Circuit → Physical Output**

The circuit was tested and troubleshooting was performed when required.

## ESP32 & Embedded Systems

The ESP32 was introduced as the central controller for connecting software with physical hardware.

The day established the basic idea that instructions written in software can be used to control electronic outputs through the microcontroller.

This concept would later be extended to sensors and motor control as the rover project progressed.

## Troubleshooting

Working with physical hardware also introduced the importance of debugging.

The practical session included checking:

- Circuit connections
- LED polarity
- Power and ground connections
- ESP32 connections
- USB/COM-port communication

This showed that building an embedded system involves both programming and physical troubleshooting.

## What I Learned

Day 1 gave me a foundation in electronics and embedded systems that would be used throughout the Lunar Rover project.

I learned how basic electronic components connect together, how an ESP32 interfaces with a circuit, and how software and hardware work together to produce a physical output.

This was the starting point for progressing from simple electronic circuits toward controlling and eventually developing the rover.
