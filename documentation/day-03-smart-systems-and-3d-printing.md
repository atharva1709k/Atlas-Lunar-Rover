# Day 3 — Smart Systems & 3D Printing

## Overview

Day 3 of the ATLAS Summer Tech School 2026 focused on understanding the different systems involved in robotics, including mechanical design, electronics, sensors, and wireless communication.

The session introduced the relationship between a robot's physical structure, electronic components, and software, while also exploring the mechanical design and sensing concepts relevant to the Lunar Rover project.

## Robotics System Architecture

The instructor explained how robotics combines different engineering disciplines to create a functioning system.

The main areas discussed included:

- Mechanical engineering
- Electronics
- Artificial intelligence and software
- Integration of mechanical and electronic systems

A four-stage development process was also introduced:

1. Building the electronic system.
2. Developing the mechanical structure.
3. Integrating the mechanical and electronic systems.
4. Completing the final assembly.

This helped establish how different components work together within a robotic system.

## Mechanical Design & 3D Printing

The session introduced the mechanical design requirements of the rover and the importance of selecting suitable materials.

Topics discussed included:

- Computer-Aided Design (CAD).
- Introduction to Fusion 360 and other CAD tools.
- Four-wheel rover chassis configuration.
- 3D printing materials such as ABS and PLA.
- Material and component selection.

The instructor also explained that mechanical design involves considering physical properties and engineering requirements rather than focusing only on appearance.

## Sensors & Robotic Awareness

An important part of the session focused on how robots collect information from their surroundings using sensors.

The instructor explained the relationship between human senses and their electronic equivalents.

Examples discussed included:

- Cameras for visual information and computer vision.
- Microphones for sound detection.
- Ultrasonic sensors for distance measurement.
- Infrared (IR) sensors for detecting infrared light.
- Piezoelectric sensors for touch and pressure-related sensing.
- Spectroscopy for identifying material and molecular characteristics.

The basic concept was that sensors collect information from the environment and send it to a processor for interpretation and decision-making.

## ESP & Wireless Communication

The session introduced the wireless communication capabilities of the ESP platform.

The instructor discussed how its built-in Wi-Fi capability can be used to establish communication between a microcontroller and a mobile device.

A mobile-phone interface for switching an LED on and off over Wi-Fi was introduced as an example of how wireless control could eventually be extended to the rover.

## Electromagnetism & Electric Motors

The session also covered the physics behind electric motors and their role in robotics.

The instructor introduced the relationship between electric and magnetic fields and explained how electric motors use electromagnetic principles to produce mechanical motion.

This connected the physics concepts discussed in the session with the motors used for rover movement.

## Practical Focus

According to the official Day 3 programme, the planned practical activities included:

- Introduction to TinkerCAD and Fusion 360.
- Designing an initial rover component, such as a bracket or wheel.
- Understanding 3D printing and slicing parameters.
- Reading sensor values using `analogRead()` and `digitalRead()`.
- Using the Serial Monitor for debugging.
- Introduction to basic obstacle-detection programming.
- Connecting an ultrasonic sensor and reading distance values.

These activities were part of the programme outline; the available recording covers the morning lecture and does not independently confirm their completion.

## What I Learned

Day 3 helped me understand how mechanical design, electronics, sensors, and software come together in robotics.

I learned about different types of sensors, the role of wireless communication in robotic control, the importance of material selection in mechanical design, and the electromagnetic principles behind electric motors.

The session provided a broader understanding of how different STEM disciplines contribute to the development of a robotic system.
