# Introduction — ATLAS Summer Tech School 2026

## About the Programme

The ATLAS Summer Tech School 2026 was a technology and innovation-focused learning programme conducted under the UGDX Summer School initiative.

Designed for Grade 9–12 students and undergraduate aspirants, the programme aimed to introduce participants to emerging technologies, interdisciplinary learning, and skills relevant to future academic and professional pathways.

The programme followed a two-week structure, combining different areas of STEM with practical learning experiences and exposure to modern technological applications.

## Programme Overview

The Summer School focused on exploring how technology, science, and innovation can be applied to real-world challenges.

The curriculum introduced participants to different technical disciplines, including:

- Physics and Mathematics
- Chemistry and Biology
- Electronics and Embedded Systems
- Arduino and Programming
- Robotics and Mechanical Design
- 3D Printing and Prototyping
- Emerging Technologies and Innovation

## The Robotic Lunar Rover Project

A central component of the programme was the development of a Robotic Lunar Rover.

The project provided an opportunity to explore the engineering principles behind robotic systems, including their mechanical structure, electronic components, control systems, and programming.

The rover development process involved learning about:

- Electronic circuits and microcontrollers.
- ESP32-based embedded systems.
- Sensors and actuators.
- Motor drivers and motor control.
- Mechanical design and rover locomotion.
- Programming and control logic.
- Hardware integration and system testing.

The project connected theoretical concepts with practical engineering applications, providing an introduction to the different stages involved in developing a robotic system.

## Interdisciplinary STEM Integration

![ATLAS Summer Tech School — Programme Overview](../DAY%200.jpg)

*Integration of different STEM disciplines across the ten-day programme.*

The Lunar Rover project brought together multiple STEM disciplines.

Physics helped explain motion, forces, electricity, and mechanical behaviour. Mathematics supported calculations and engineering measurements. Chemistry and material science introduced concepts related to batteries and material properties. Electronics and programming provided the foundation for sensing, processing, and controlling the rover.

This interdisciplinary approach demonstrated how different areas of knowledge contribute to solving a single engineering challenge.



## Programme Structure

The programme was organised into a sequence of daily learning themes, progressing from foundational concepts to rover development and integration.

The overall learning journey covered:

1. Orientation and hands-on exploration.
2. Engineering and embedded systems.
3. Smart systems and 3D printing.
4. Vehicle dynamics and autonomous driving.
5. The World of STEAM.
6. Rover design and development.
7. 3D printing and prototyping.
8. Assembly and embedded systems.
9. Future technologies and innovation.
10. Final showcase and Lunar Rover challenge.
